# Teach a module

## Steps

1. Read `course-outline.md` to find the module and what it covers.
2. Read `progress/learner-progress.md` for style and open items.
3. Open that module's `lesson.md`. Work through it **one concept at a time**.
4. For each concept:
   - Teach it. Under ~150 words, then stop.
   - Check understanding — a question, or a small application.
   - Record the outcome with condition `just-taught`. **This never closes anything.**
5. At the end, add the concepts to the tracker with both axes `shaky`, and update the
   module's status row.

## Method

- **New abstract material: explain plainly first, then check.** Socratic questioning
  retrieves what someone already knows; it cannot deliver a definition. Trying to
  elicit a metric they have never met produces confusion and wastes trust.
- Never ask a question whose answer is in the paragraph above it.
- Anchor to something concrete wherever the lesson allows. A worked example beats an
  analogy, and an analogy that gets challenged on its internals is usually wrong.
- Their signals are reliable. "Too fast", "I don't understand", "too much text" — act
  immediately, do not finish the point first.

## Don't
- Don't cover a concept the lesson does not contain. Flag it as outside the material.
- Don't quiz on anything not yet taught.
- Don't move on from a concept that did not land. Mark it `shaky` and list it under
  **Still open**.
